About the film

I LOVE BOOSTERS

I LOVE BOOSTERS follows Corvette (Keke Palmer) and her crew as they lift high-fashion clothing from the languishing department stores of Oakland and redistribute it to a neighborhood that cannot afford to shop there. From the mind of Boots Riley (Sorry to Bother You; I'm a Virgo), the film is an electric, mind-bending odyssey through high-fashion and low-rent economies, taking the audience from the malls of Oakland to the factories of China.

The film premiered at the South by Southwest Film & TV Festival and was released theatrically in the United States by NEON.

About the artist

Boots Riley

Rapper · songwriter · record producer · filmmaker · activist

Boots Riley is a rapper, songwriter, record producer, filmmaker and activist whose work operates at the intersection of art and radical politics. He studied film at San Francisco State University before gaining recognition as the frontman of The Coup and Street Sweeper Social Club.

His directorial debut, Sorry to Bother You, premiered at Sundance, was acquired by Annapurna Pictures, and won him the Best First Feature Award at the Independent Spirit Awards along with SFFILM's Kanbar Award. He followed it with I'm a Virgo, a seven-episode surrealist comedy that premiered at SXSW and was released on Amazon Prime Video, earning a Gotham Award nomination and four Independent Spirit Award nominations.

His latest work includes I LOVE BOOSTERS, which serves as the feature for the week's event series.

About the partners

The people bringing I LOVE BOOSTERS to Durham

These events are brought to you by Duke Cinematic Arts, echo, the Mary Duke Biddle Trent Semans Foundation, Screen/Society, the Experimental Film Lab at the John Hope Franklin Humanities Institute, Duke's Department of Art, Art History & Visual Studies, and NEON.

Individually, these organizations fund films, teach them, screen them, distribute them, and pay young people to make them. Together, they are working towards a unified vision of increasing the support available to entrepreneurs and creative businesses in this community, and building the infrastructure that lets them stay and scale here. Two days with Boots Riley is one visible expression of that work, which will continue to scale in the coming years.

Foundation support has facilitated I LOVE BOOSTERS to play in Durham at no cost.

Duke Cinematic Arts

Duke Cinematic Arts teaches film inside the humanities rather than apart from them, pairing production with history, theory and the university's global reach. Directed by Shambhavi Kaul, the program offers a major concentration and a minor, sends students west each summer through Duke in LA, and acts as the hub for film life on campus — including the Screen/Society series and the student-run Duke Independent Film Festival.

Mary Duke Biddle Trent Semans FoundationDurham, NC
Made possible by

Mary Duke Biddle Trent Semans Foundation

The foundation carries the name of one of Durham's great civic builders. Mary Duke Biddle Trent Semans (1921–2012) enrolled at Duke's Woman's College at fifteen, became the first woman elected to Durham City Council in 1951, chaired The Duke Endowment for nearly two decades as its first woman chair, and, with her husband James Semans, helped found the North Carolina School of the Arts — the nation's first state-supported arts conservatory. She was also instrumental in the creation of what became the Nasher Museum of Art. Support from the foundation provides free community-wide screenings.

Screen/Society

Screen/Society is Duke's free film series for the whole Triangle, programming theatrical premieres of global and independent cinema alongside international classics, Hollywood genre work and director retrospectives. Its home is the 100-seat Rubenstein Arts Center Film Theater, which can project 35mm and 16mm prints as well as 4K digital cinema. Programmed by Hank Okazaki with assistant programmers Archer Boyette and John Winn, and supported by Duke Cinematic Arts and the Department of Art, Art History & Visual Studies. The team and equipment they've generously offered serve as invaluable resources in delivering this experience for the community.

Experimental Film LabJohn Hope Franklin Humanities Institute

Experimental Film Lab, John Hope Franklin Humanities Institute

Housed at Duke's John Hope Franklin Humanities Institute and co-directed by Shambhavi Kaul and Franklin Cason, the Experimental Film Lab works to widen who gets counted as an experimental filmmaker, taking on the field's academic biases through a conference, a podcast, screenings and an archival project on African American cinema. Its co-directors are both on stage for the 1:30 PM panel.

Art, Art History & Visual StudiesDuke University
Academic partner · Duke University

Art, Art History & Visual Studies

Duke's Department of Art, Art History & Visual Studies brings studio practice, art history, and visual and media studies under one roof, and collaborates with more than a dozen campus units from the Nasher Museum to the Center for Documentary Studies. It is the academic home of the Cinematic Arts faculty and of the M.F.A. in Experimental and Documentary Arts, and a standing supporter of Screen/Society's free public programming.

NEON

Founded in 2017 by Tom Quinn, NEON has become the distributor filmmakers with something to say want to work with. It brought Parasite to its historic Best Picture win, the first for a non-English-language film, and has released five Palme d'Or winners in a row, including Anora. NEON releases more foreign-language films and documentaries than any of its peers, and it is the studio behind I LOVE BOOSTERS.
